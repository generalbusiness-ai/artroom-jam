## 2 What the lead now does

### In general

Bars are the lead's own, counted from its arrival. After the entrance, it
plays in passes of two bars (the theme's length, and never fewer than two).
**[code]**

- Bar 0, the entrance: silent for half a bar, then the theme's most sung note,
  struck an eighth late (step 10) at velocity 120, bent up a whole tone and
  held 14 sixteenths into the next bar. The old lead played one whole note at
  velocity 84 from the downbeat. **[code]**
- Bar 1, the tail: the theme's last two notes, the first slid down into, the
  second bent up into and held to the end of the bar. **[code]**
- The first bar of each pass, the phrase: an answer to the synth's motif. It
  keeps the theme's rhythm and turns its contour upside down within the same
  range, so where the synth's hum rises, the lead falls. On odd passes it is
  moved up a fourth or a fifth in the key (the seed chooses). The downbeat is
  left empty. Long notes are laid back an eighth, bent or slid into, at full
  volume; short notes are ghosted at velocity 64. From a sixteenth before beat
  4 comes the flourish, in turn: a trill, a fast run down to the last note, or
  a held scream two octaves above the synth's hum. **[code]**
- The second bar of each pass, the show-off: a ghosted note, then a run of
  seven sixteenths up the key from a sixteenth before beat 2, landing an
  octave up on a note bent in, held for half a bar and left to hang into the
  next bar. **[code]**
- Every fourth pass: a silent bar, then the biggest phrase, a run of fourteen
  sixteenths two octaves up the key to the scream, at velocity 127, bent and
  held for a bar. **[code]**
- Velocities run from 64 to 127 and the accents rise a little each pass.
  **[code]**

## 6 G: a mood phrase in the room steers the whole band

The direction (hugh, 2026-10-07, through the local colleague): "overall jam
style could be steered with a mood-phrase in the room, e.g. 'detroit techno'
or 'lonesome country'."

### What changed, by file

- `src/record.ts`: a new entry kind, `mood`, with the phrase as said and who
  said it. It takes effect by the same rule as every entry: the first bar
  boundary at least one lookahead after it was recorded. `activeAt` now gives
  `mood`, the latest mood entry in effect, and `style`, the style of the latest
  mood in effect that was understood. A mood's tempo nudge is part of the
  tempo map: the tempo from a mood's effect bar is the interpretation's tempo
  plus the nudge, held between 60 and 180, and a later interpretation keeps
  the nudge. Note events may carry `late` (swing, a fraction of a step) and
  `tone` (a timbre preset). **[code]**
- `src/mood.ts` (new): the style, the lexicon, the two reference moods, the
  fallback, the caption text and `inMood`, which plays an interpretation in
  the mood's mode. **[code]**
- `src/music.ts`: keys can be played in mixolydian, pentatonic (minor) and
  blues as well as major and minor; the tonic triad takes its third from the
  mode. **[code]**
- `src/players/*.ts`: each player takes the style as a fourth argument
  (neutral when absent) and has a vocabulary per mood (below). The bass part,
  which nobody takes, ignores it. **[code]**
- `src/players/player.ts`: `feel`, what every part shares in a mood: each
  voice's timbre preset, and swing, which plays every off-beat eighth late.
  **[code]**
- `src/band.ts`: `mood(band, time, text)` records a mood. At each bar the band
  gives every player the interpretation in the mood's mode and the style in
  effect at the bar it is playing for, and applies `feel` to what comes back.
  **[code]**
- `src/render.ts`: presets within the existing model. Bass: bright opens the
  filter wider and drives the saw; warm is a triangle with a low filter and a
  slow decay. Stab: bright is wider open, driven and shorter; warm is two
  triangles with a softer attack and a longer ring. Arpeggio: bright opens
  further and drives the square; warm is a triangle pluck. Lead: warm has less
  drive, a darker filter, a slower and deeper vibrato and a softer echo; bright
  is the lead as in section 1. Events marked `late` are placed that fraction
  of a step late. With no preset every voice renders as before. **[code]**
  **[run]**
- `src/clip.ts`, `scripts/clip.ts`: a plan item may be a mood; the mood clip
  plan; the script renders both clips. **[code]**
- `page/`: a text box, "say it", records a mood at once and says the bar it
  takes effect; the bar line shows the mood and the key in the mood's mode;
  the captions show "the room says: ..." from the mood's effect bar.
  **[code]** In the headless browser at 375 pixels wide: "detriot techno" said
  about 3 s after "sing" showed "It takes effect at bar 3"; at bar 3 the bar line read
  "111 bpm · G# minor · detriot techno" and the caption read "the room says:
  detriot techno ("detriot" heard as "detroit")"; no sideways scroll. One

### What a mood changes

| Parameter | Neutral (no mood) | detroit techno | lonesome country |
|---|---|---|---|
| tempo nudge | 0 | +6 | -14 |
| swing (steps late, off-beat eighths) | 0 | 0 | 0.6 |
| mode | as interpreted | minor | mixolydian |
| density | 1 | 1.5 | 0.5 |
| note length | 1 | 0.5 | 2 |
| timbre | none | bright: bass, stab, arpeggio, lead | warm: bass, stab, arpeggio, lead |
| synth | groove (as in J2) | drive | boom-chick |
| percussion | floor (as in J2) | drive | train |
| lead | rockstar (section 2) | stab | mournful |

**[code]**

The vocabularies: **[code]**

- Synth, drive: the bass on every sixteenth, hardest on the beat (the groove
  itself is four on the floor), the octave on each off-beat; stabs on the
  off-beats and the last sixteenth of beat 3; the arpeggio from the second
  groove bar on 12 sixteenths a bar at density 1.5, its filter opening twice as
  fast.
- Synth, boom-chick: a long root on beat 1 and a long fifth on beat 3 (6
  sixteenths at length 2), the tonic chord on 2 and 4, and from the third
  groove bar two of the theme's notes on off-beat eighths at density 0.5.
- Percussion, drive: kick on every beat, claps on 2 and 4, a closed hat on
  every sixteenth and an open hat on each off-beat; the sung rhythm's low
  onsets on the low tom.
- Percussion, train: kick on 1 and 3, a soft clap on 2 and 4, hats on the
  off-beat eighths only at density under 1, a two-tom pickup every eighth bar.
- Lead, stab: two or three short hard notes of the theme a bar, off the beat,
  a five-note stutter every fourth bar, no glides; the entrance is two stabs
  in the second half of the bar.
- Lead, mournful: the rockstar's notes, with velocities squeezed into 60 to
  85, long and glided notes falling into place from a step above over two
  steps, screams an octave lower, and notes held longer up to the next note.

### The lexicon and the fallback

The words, each setting some parameters over neutral, in the order said:
detroit, techno, driving, dark, bright, warm, lonesome, country, blues, sad,
happy, rock, slow, fast, swing, sparse, busy. The two reference moods are
whole phrases and are used as written when the understood words are exactly
"detroit techno" or "lonesome country". **[code]**

Fallback: a phrase is lower-cased and split into words. A word not in the
lexicon is read as the nearest word that is, by edit distance (a swapped pair
of letters counts as one edit), if it is within one edit in four letters (at
least one); otherwise it is ignored. If no word is understood, the style stays
as it was. The caption states each fallback. **[code]** Confirmed by the
tests: **[run]**

- "detriot tecno" is read as "detroit techno", the reference mood; caption
  `detriot tecno ("detriot" heard as "detroit"; "tecno" heard as "techno")`.
- "lonesome yodel" is read as "lonesome"; caption `lonesome yodel ("yodel"
  not understood)`.
- "polka" after "detroit techno" leaves detroit techno in force (style and
  tempo); caption `polka (not understood; the style stays as it was)`.
