# Spike J2: riffing

The band in software, from a sung theme to a clip that can be recorded and a
page that plays live. Everything is local and in memory; the platform is not
involved.

Each claim is labelled: **[code]** read from code, **[run]** confirmed by a
run in the build container on 2026-10-07, **[inferred]** reasoned but not
confirmed.

## 1 What is built

### By file

- `src/record.ts`: the record, an in-memory log of entries (sing, interpret,
  take, pattern, say), each with `seq`, `time` and `by`. The timing rule is
  pure functions over the log: `schedule` gives every entry its effect bar
  and builds the tempo map; `activeAt(log, bar)` gives the tempo, the
  interpretation, each taken part with the pattern it plays and which bar of
  that pattern, and what is said at that bar. A pattern states `bars` and
  `follows` (the interpretation), never a start bar; it repeats until the
  same part's next pattern takes effect. The lookahead is `rules.lookahead`,
  1 bar by default. **[code]**
- `src/interpret.ts`: a tune's notes to an interpretation (tempo, a 4/4 grid
  of sixteenth steps, the quantized theme, a key); a rhythm phrase to tempo,
  grid and rhythm, keeping the key and theme from before. **[code]**
- `src/music.ts`: keys, scales, snapping a pitch into a key, a seeded random
  number generator. **[code]**
- `src/phrases.ts`: the theme and the rhythm phrase from spike J0, as given.
  **[code]**
- `src/players/`: `synth.ts`, `percussion.ts`, `lead.ts` and `bass.ts`, each a
  deterministic function from (interpretation, bar, seed) to a pattern and,
  now and then, a line of banter; each has a `.prompt.md` beside it saying in
  one paragraph what it plays. `player.ts` holds what they share; `index.ts`
  lists them and the arrival order (synth, percussion, lead). **[code]**
- `src/band.ts`: the band writing to the record. A sing is recorded with the
  synth's interpretation at the same time; the first sing also records the
  synth's take and fixes when the others arrive, one every eight bars. At
  each bar boundary the band records the takes that are due and each taken
  part's next pattern and banter. **[code]**
- `src/clip.ts`: the clip's plan (the theme at time 0, the rhythm phrase half
  way through bar 17) and `perform`, which plays a plan through the band.
  **[code]**
- `src/render.ts`: the offline synthesizer: naive oscillators, one-pole
  filters, envelopes, a sine kick, noise hats and clap, sine toms, a small
  Schroeder reverb on a send, mono 16-bit WAV encoding at 44.1 kHz. **[code]**
- `scripts/clip.ts`: writes `out/j2-clip.wav` and `out/j2-log.json` (the log
  that produced it). `out/` is ignored by git. **[code]**
- `page/index.html`, `page/app.ts`, `page/live.ts`, `page/view.ts`: the live
  page. `live.ts` drives the band from the audio clock; `view.ts` computes
  what the stage shows; `app.ts` renders each bar with the same synthesizer as
  the clip and schedules it as an audio buffer at the bar's start, and draws
  the stage. **[code]**
- `scripts/serve-page.ts`: a small local server for the page. It serves the
  repository and strips the types from `.ts` files as it serves them, so the
  page loads the same modules as the clip with no build step. **[code]**

No dependencies were added. **[code]**

### By test

Run with `npm test`. All 30 pass; figures are at the end of this section.
**[run]**

`test/record.test.ts` (part A)

- an entry takes effect at the first bar boundary at least one lookahead
  after it was recorded
- an effect bar is never before the effect bar of the entry before it
- a lowered lookahead brings the effect bar forward
- an effect bar is computed with the tempo active at the entry time
- a tempo change recorded for a future bar does not move earlier bars
- a pattern plays from its effect bar, repeats, and is replaced by the next
- a pattern is silent until its part has been taken
- entries must be recorded in time order

`test/interpret.test.ts` (part B)

- the theme quantizes to eight notes and names its tempo and key
- the rhythm phrase sets tempo and grid and keeps the key and theme
- a tempo is 120 and unclear when the onsets say too little
- a tempo snaps into 100 to 130
- the key is the most common pitch class, minor unless the major third is
  sung more

`test/players.test.ts` (part C)

- every player is deterministic and stays on its grid
- the synth hums the theme back first, then plays the groove with no drums
  and no lead
- percussion keeps the floor, claps on 2 and 4, and fills every eighth bar
- percussion follows the sung rhythm when one is active
- the lead enters with long notes, then plays the motif and answers it
- each player now and then says a line, always on arrival

`test/band.test.ts` (parts C and D, the clip's log)

- in the clip the agents take synth, percussion and lead, eight bars apart,
  and nobody takes bass
- in the clip the synth hums the theme alone, then grooves
- in the clip the rhythm phrase takes effect at its effect bar with the tempo
  change
- the clip lasts about 45 seconds
- no pattern entry states a start bar

`test/render.test.ts` (part D)

- four rendered bars have the right length and peak under 0 dBFS
- the lead is silent before it arrives

`test/page.test.ts` (part E, the page's data functions)

- the live clock schedules each bar once, in order, before it starts
- a button press takes effect at its effect bar, and the stage shows it
  waiting until then

## 2 How to produce the clip and open the page

Both need the JavaScript runtime at version 22.18 or later, which runs
`.ts` files directly. Nothing needs installing.

```
node scripts/clip.ts
```

writes `out/j2-clip.wav` and prints its length and peak. In the build
container it printed: bars 1 to 20, 45.2 s of bars and 47.7 s in all (the
last 2.5 s are the release and reverb tail), peak -1.00 dBFS, 57 log
entries, rendered in 1.1 s. **[run]**

```
node scripts/serve-page.ts
```

then open `http://127.0.0.1:8080/page/` and press "sing". Press "sing a
rhythm" whenever you like. `PORT=9000` changes the port. **[code]**

## 4 Stand-ins and limits

- No microphone. Both buttons and the clip use the transcriptions from spike
  J0 in `src/phrases.ts`. **[code]**
- No model. The players are deterministic functions; the prompt files say
  what a model-backed player would be asked to play, but nothing reads them.
  **[code]**
- No platform. The record is a plain array in memory. The page's record lives
  in the page and is lost on reload. **[code]**
- The synth interprets at the same instant as the sing, so the sing and its
  interpretation share an effect bar. A real agent would take time to reply,
  and its interpretation could land a bar later. **[code]** **[inferred]**
- The band's clock is ideal in the clip. On the page it is the audio clock;
  the band records at most 0.5 s before each bar boundary and the bar's
  sound is scheduled at once. In the headless browser run nothing was late
  and there were no console errors, but no sound was heard. **[run]**
- The page was run in a headless browser for 46 seconds: the bar counter,
  tempo, key, waiting notice, figures appearing at bars 1, 9 and 17, the
  nodding head and the captions all behaved as the tests say. A press of
  "sing a rhythm" on beat 2 of bar 9 showed "takes effect at bar 11", and
  the tempo read 119 at the later checks. **[run]** Two screenshots were looked at; the
  page also fits a 375 pixel wide screen with no sideways scroll. **[run]**
- Tempo. The theme's spacing (median 0.33 s, read as eighth notes) implies
  91 beats per minute, which snaps to 100 and is refined by grid fit to 105.
  The reference performance was about 120. The rhythm phrase gives 119.
  **[run]**
- Key. The simplest histogram makes the theme G# minor (G# is sung three
  times; neither B nor C is sung). The arpeggio and the lead move the theme's
  A and D into the key (to G# and C#). The synth's hum-back keeps the pitches
  as sung. Whether that is the right key is a listener's call. **[run]**
  **[inferred]**
- The theme fills two bars at 105 (its last note is on step 18), so the synth
  hums it back over two bars, not one as the brief said. **[run]**
- The renderer's oscillators alias and its filters are gentle; it is a
  sketch of a techno sound, not a finished one. It is mono. **[code]**
- When the rhythm phrase arrives the lead keeps counting its own bars, so it
  carries on from where it was; percussion reads the rhythm from its next
  pattern. Nothing restarts. **[code]**

## 5 What is owed

- A listen to `out/j2-clip.wav` and to the page by the person, with
  judgements on the groove, the key choice and the balance. Nobody has heard
  either. **[inferred]**
- Model-backed players that read the prompt files and the interpretation and
  return patterns of the same shape, with banter. **[inferred]**
- The real room: the log written as the room's record, the timing rule run
  by the room, and the page reading the room. The entry shapes here were
  kept plain so they can move across. **[inferred]**
- The J1 timing measurement (commit to effect on a deployed room), which
  decides whether a 1 bar lookahead is enough. **[inferred]**
- A rule change in the record. The test of the monotone rule lowers the
  lookahead part way through a session, which `Rules.lookaheadChanges`
  allows. In a room this would be an entry of its own; the brief lists no
  such entry kind, so it stays in the rules here. **[code]**
- Things from A to F not done as written: the synth's hum-back lasts two bars
  rather than one, for the reason in section 4. Everything else in A to F is
  built. **[run]**
- Review in a room, once the platform can host this repository. **[inferred]**

## 6 Decisions followed

- The jam: a person hums; an agent at a synth interprets and plays back; more
  agents arrive in a fixed order: synth (the whole groove, bass included, no
  drum kit, no lead melody), then percussion, then a lead (guitar here). A
  bass part exists but nobody takes it. Another phrase is picked up by the
  band. **[code]**
- Visuals: a white room, a table, instruments that look like instruments,
  players who nod on the beat, banter as captions. **[code]** **[run]**
- Timing: a change is recorded at once and is active from its effect bar,
  the first bar boundary at least one lookahead after it was recorded; every
  effect bar is computed with what is active at the entry's time; an effect
  bar is never before the one before it. **[code]** **[run]**
- A pattern states its length in bars and the interpretation it follows,
  never a start bar. **[code]** **[run]**
- A rhythm phrase is a second sing with a kind of tune or rhythm; an
  interpret after a rhythm sing sets tempo and grid and keeps the key.
  **[code]** **[run]**
- The lookahead is a value of the rules, 1 bar by default. **[code]**
- Players arrive one every eight bars. **[run]**
- Local and in memory; the platform is not involved. **[code]**
- No framework and no added dependencies; tests with the runtime's own test
  runner. **[code]**
- The clip synthesizes symbolic THEME/RHYTHM fixtures from J0's reference
  transcription of a published comedy sketch excerpt, and variations on
  that fixture; no source audio samples are used. These are not Hugh's five
  private supplied recordings, and this note supplies no ownership or
  publication-rights proof for the reference fixture. **[code]**

Decisions of this spike, not in the brief:

- A player's bar argument is its own bar count from its arrival, so that
  "fill every eighth bar" and "entering with long notes" have a start.
  **[code]**
- The page renders each bar with the clip's synthesizer and plays it as an
  audio buffer, so the page and the clip sound the same. **[code]**
- Tempo is estimated in two steps (spacing, then grid fit within 6 beats per
  minute); the thresholds for "unclear" are in `src/interpret.ts`. **[code]**
